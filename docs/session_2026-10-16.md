## 4. Exact local search (new; paper §8.3)

`src/exact_local.py`. Proposition (paper, "Exact local search"): once the cyclic order and the
step sequence are fixed, the admissible hidden directions form a finite union of sets defined by
linear equations and inequalities; so for each k the existence of a local history with k hidden
rays (distinct directions) is decidable by finitely many linear feasibility problems.

Computed (tolerances: congruences 1e-9, rays at least 1e-6 apart; every history re-checked in
floating point):

| Vertex | Result |
|---|---|
| running pattern, v* | minimum 1 hidden ray; the paper's history |
| Horned Owl, rays 22.5, 112.5, 135, 225 | minimum 3, in any directions (the paper had only multiples of 22.5°) |
| Bern–Hayes crossover (all four vertices, congruent) | no history with ≤ 4 hidden rays (k = 4: 5,229 search nodes); k = 5 ran 7.5 h without finishing and was stopped |

## 5. Question 25: leads (proved by hand below, not machine-checked)

1. **Slides are Euclid steps.** Take a degree-4 flat vertex with consecutive sectors
   (θ4, θ1, θ2, θ3), rays s1 < s2 < s3 < s4 with θ1 = s2 - s1, and θ1 < θ2. The step
   {s1 erased, h = s2 + θ1 created} satisfies (P) (two rays), (L) (the wedge from s1 to h
   contains only s2, and h = 2 s2 - s1), and (C) (both moving pieces have an edge on the fold
   line and angle < π, so neither straddles it). The new sectors are (θ4 + θ1, θ1, θ2 - θ1, θ3):
   a Euclid step on (θ1, θ2).
2. **Collinear pairs at degree 4.** A degree-4 flat vertex has a collinear pair iff two adjacent
   sectors are equal (rays s_i, s_{i+2} are collinear iff θ_i + θ_{i+1} = π, and θ_i + θ_{i+2} = π).
3. **An invariant for a restricted class.** Let G(S) be the subgroup of ℝ generated by π and the
   differences of the directions in S. If a step makes at least one ray, then G(before) ⊆ G(after):
   reversed, the step satisfies the wedge condition with respect to the after-state, so every ray
   it erases is ±x + 2s for a ray x it makes and an integer combination s of after-state rays.
   A step between two 4-ray states makes as many rays as it erases, so at least one, and so does
   its reverse; hence it preserves G. A 4-ray flat state with a collinear pair x, x + π has its
   other two rays at y and σ_x(y) or y + π, so its G is ⟨π, y - x⟩. For the crossover,
   G = ⟨π/4, θ⟩ with θ ≈ 50.71°. If θ ∉ ℚπ these groups cannot be equal (π/4 = m(y - x) + nπ
   forces y - x ∈ ℚπ, and then θ ∈ ℚπ). So **no sequence of steps through 4-ray states joins the
   crossover vertex to a 4-ray state with a collinear pair.** Here θ ∉ ℚπ: the file's coordinates
   give tan θ = 11/9 (and the fourth direction is 45° + θ, with tangent −10), so
   cos 2θ = −20/101, which is rational and not in {0, ±1/2, ±1}; by Niven's theorem 2θ ∉ ℚπ. The exact search, which also covers states with more rays, found
   no history with ≤ 4 hidden rays. A proof for all histories needs control of steps into and out
   of states with more than four rays, in particular steps that make only hidden rays, which
   introduce a free direction.
