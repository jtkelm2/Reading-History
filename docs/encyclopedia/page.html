Degree-4 Vertex Encyclopedia

A flat degree-4 vertex is fixed by two adjacent sector angles, since θ₃ = 180° − θ₁ and θ₄ = 180° − θ₂. Rotating and mirroring the vertex leaves one triangle of shapes, θ₁ ≤ θ₂ ≤ 90°: θ₁ is the smallest sector and θ₂ its smaller neighbour. Every local history in the tables needs a condition on the angles, so it lives on a line or at a point of that triangle, called its stratum. Pick a stratum to see the histories that are realizable with paper (rigidly, or with reverse folds), step by step.

What this page is computed from

The degree-4 tables (at most 4 hidden rays, scaffold lines included) and their exact realizability cells, exported by tools/encyclopedia_export.py. Realizability along a stratum is exact: the cell trees split on forms that are affine along the line, so their intervals have rational endpoints, and every crossing, corpus vertex and isolated realizable point is evaluated exactly. Histories that differ by a rotation or a mirror image are shown once.

Checked by tools/encyclopedia_check.py: at 480 random vertices and at all 66 special vertices, the histories on this page are exactly those the solver's lookup finds (with the physical filter), modulo the vertex's own symmetry. The page's replay of each history was compared with the Python replay at 2,658 points, with no difference.

A history is hidden as dominated when another on the same stratum needs fewer hidden rays and is at least as realizable (rigid, then reverse folds, then none) at every vertex of the stratum.
