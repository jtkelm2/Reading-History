Degree-4 vertex encyclopedia

The ways to fold a flat-foldable degree-4 origami vertex from a flat sheet, one line at a time, and whether paper can actually perform them. Each vertex shape is a point (θ₁, θ₂) of a triangle; each folding history works only on a line or at a point of it, its stratum. Pick a stratum, or enter a vertex, to see its histories step by step.

Definitions
Vertex
Four creases a₀, a₁, a₂, a₃ counterclockwise around a point, with sector angles θ₁ from a₀ to a₁, …, θ₄ from a₃ to a₀. Flat-foldability (Kawasaki) forces θ₃ = 180° − θ₁ and θ₄ = 180° − θ₂. Up to rotation and reflection every shape is one point of the triangle 0 < θ₁ ≤ θ₂ ≤ 90°.
History
A sequence of steps starting from the flat sheet. A step picks an even number of rays whose images in the current folded state lie on one line, and folds along that line: rays that are not creases become creases, and rays that are creases are erased.
Scaffold crease
A crease that a history makes and later erases. Drawn dashed in orange; ✕ marks an erasure. Strata are coloured by the fewest scaffold creases among their physical histories.
Free line
A step that makes only new scaffold creases has no existing crease to fix its line. Its direction λ (mod 180°) is then given by an affine formula in the crease directions a₁, a₂, a₃, measured counterclockwise from a₀ = 0°.
Rigid
Realizable with every step a half-turn of a block of layers about the fold line, all moving layers turning the same way and no paper passing through paper (the layered model).
Reverse folds
As rigid, but the two halves of a flap may turn in opposite senses, as in inside and outside reverse folds. A history is physical where it is realizable rigidly or with reverse folds.
Stratum
Every history in the tables needs a linear condition on θ₁, θ₂, so it lives on a line or at a point of the triangle: its stratum. Realizability can change along a stratum.
Tables
The enumerated histories with at most four scaffold creases, up to rotation and reflection. Most are never physical; only their number is shown.
Corpus vertex ◆
A vertex shape that occurs in a collection of published crease patterns. Hover over one in the triangle to see which.

The model is that of Sequential realizability of a flat vertex.

How this page was computed

From the degree-4 tables (at most four scaffold creases, free lines included) and their realizability cells, exported by tools/encyclopedia_export.py. Along a stratum the cell trees split on forms that are affine on the line, so their intervals have rational endpoints, and realizability is evaluated exactly at every crossing, corpus vertex and isolated realizable point. Corpus vertices are evaluated at the rational coordinates recorded for them; where a true angle is irrational, such as arctan ½, the record is an approximation. Angles are displayed to one decimal place. Histories that differ by a rotation or a reflection are shown once.

Checked by tools/encyclopedia_check.py: at 480 random vertices and at all 66 special vertices, the histories on this page are exactly those the solver’s lookup finds (with the physical filter), modulo the vertex’s own symmetry. The page’s replay of each history was compared with the Python replay at 2,658 points, with no difference.

A history is hidden as dominated when another on the same stratum needs fewer scaffold creases and is at least as realizable (rigid, then reverse folds, then none) at every vertex of the stratum.
